My dad was an IT guy. Once, when I was a kid, he told me: "You can do anything and make anything you want that can run on a computer."

He was right. I'd like that on the record before I start yelling.

I've talked about the Warrior Codex here before. It's the app our cat game runs in. It tracks every cat, every wound, every grudge, every dice roll, and there's a little player app, so you can sit down, open a window, and play. I made that. Me. It didn't exist and now it does.

This morning I wanted to play it. That's all. One hour of roleplay in the thing I built for exactly that purpose.

Instead, the player app opened once. Once! And then never again. First launch, beautiful. Second launch, nothing. And this was after the latest update. And the update before that. Two updates in a row whose entire job was to make things better, and the one thing I needed, the literal bare minimum of "the app opens", was the thing that broke.

So I spent the whole ass morning on it. I didn't play or write. I stared at a window that wouldn't open and typed in all caps at an AI. I called it a piece of metal. I'm not proud of it, but I'm also not taking it back.

Here's the part nobody tells you, and the part my dad left out. The full sentence is:

"You can make anything you want that can run on a computer, but not without crying a little bit, yelling at a screen, pacing around your house thinking about injections and Python, and doubting your ability to do things and solve problems."

That's the silent half. That's the fine print.

Because the thing about making stuff is that the making part is maybe a third of it. The rest is this. It's the bug that makes no sense. It's the "it worked yesterday." It's finding out the part you were sure was broken is completely fine and the actual problem is somewhere you weren't even looking. It's walking laps around your kitchen muttering about update loops like a person who needs to be checked on.

And the doubt is the worst of it. The bug itself is just a bug. But around hour three, a little voice shows up and goes, "Maybe you're not actually someone who can do this." As if the app not opening is a verdict on me as a person, and not just a computer being a computer.

It isn't a verdict. I know it isn't. Everybody who has ever made anything that runs on a computer has had this exact morning. My dad definitely had this morning, probably hundreds of them. He just gave me the inspirational half of the quote and let me find the rest out myself. Classic dad move.

So this is coding. This is making things. You get the power to build literally whatever you can imagine, and the price is that sometimes you lose a whole morning to a window that won't open, and you cry a little, and you yell at a robot.

And then you fix it. Or you will. I'm still in the "will" part as I write this.

But I'm going to fix it. And then I'm going to play my stupid cat game for an hour, in the Warrior Codex, and it's going to open twice.
