For about six years, I was with my ex-husband, who thought the things I love were a problem.

Not a quirk. Not "eh, not really my thing." A problem. The making, the games, the hours spent building stuff nobody asked for. To them, all of it was a hindrance. It was holding us back. It was bad for our life together.

When you hear that for long enough, from someone you love, you start to believe it. You do the thing you love in the gaps, a little guilty, like you're sneaking a snack. You think maybe they're right. Maybe it is a waste of time. Maybe a real adult would put it down.

And honestly, in the moment, it sometimes felt right. That's how it gets you. It sounds responsible. It sounds like the sensible thing a grown-up says.

It wasn't.

Then it changed

Four years ago I got with my husband, and he supports me. He's happy I love the things I love, and he loves me for who I actually am, not a version of me with the interesting parts sanded off.

And look what happened. I make things out loud now. This site exists. The games on it exist. And then I did the scary one: I started my own business.

I got my first client in the first week.

The first week! The thing I was told was a waste of my time turned into a business with a client in seven days. I'm still a little mad about it, in a good way.

The grown-up part

I know. You can't spend every waking moment doing only what you want. There are bills and jobs and dishes and people who need you. I'm not telling you to quit everything and go live in a cabin making pottery. (Unless that's your thing. Then go.)

What I am saying is: don't let anybody take it away from you. Don't let anyone convince you that the thing that makes you happy is a flaw you need to manage. Make room for it. An hour. One night a week. Ten minutes before bed. Protect it like it matters, because it does.

And if someone in your life keeps telling you the things you love are the problem, it's worth asking whether the things are really the problem.

The seed

Here's how I've started thinking about it. Wherever you find joy in life, that's where the seed is. Not the whole tree. Just the seed. It doesn't look like much, and it's easy for someone to tell you it's nothing, or step on it, or tell you to plant something more practical instead.

But everything that's grown in my life lately grew out of the stuff I was told to stop doing.

So keep the seed. Water it when you can. Find people who'll help you water it, not people who want to pave over it. Pursuing the things you love is always worth the risk, even when someone makes giving up on them sound like the right call.

Mine took my entire twenties. Six years of being told to put it down, four years with someone who helped me pick it back up, and one week to land a client. lol.
