How to play

The game

Mark between four and ten numbers on the board. The machine then draws twenty numbers out of eighty. Every number you marked that comes out is a catch.

The table follows your marks

There is no spot button. Mark a fifth number and you are playing the five-spot table; unmark it and you are back on the four. The PAYS panel always shows the table you would be paid from if you drew right now. Below four marks there is no table, and DRAW stays dark.

Your bet

BET + and − BET step the wager. The 5¢ and 25¢ buttons choose how big a step is. Every win on the table is shown in money at the bet you have set, so raising the bet raises every line on the paytable with it.

This game pays

—

Catch counts that pay at your current marks and bet.

Maximum award

$800.00

Per game round, base win and features combined. If a round would pay more than this, it pays this.

Eggs — the short run

Before the draw, 4 eggs are placed on numbers you did not mark. Every egg that comes out hatches. Hatch enough and a screen of covered eggs appears — open one, and it holds your number of free games:

The eggs sit on numbers you didn't take, so an egg coming out is always a coin that missed your board. That is the trade.

Chests — the long run, and the Hoard

5 chests are placed the same way, never on your numbers and never on an egg.

Land eggs and chests in the same game and you are awarded both, added together.

The Hoard Bonus

Every chest out is the rarest thing on this machine, and it is the one award that cannot pay nothing. 4 chests come up; you open one; it holds 10× to 40× your bet, in cash, and the smallest of them is still 10×.

What is inside each one is shuffled and locked before you are shown anything, so every chest is equally likely to hold every amount — there is no way to pick well and no way to pick badly. When you open one, the others open too, so you can see it.

Free games

A free game deals the same ticket you were playing, at the same bet, and costs nothing. The room changes while they run.

A free game is a full game. The eggs and chests are still there, so a free game can win more free games — the counter goes up in the middle of a run — and it can open the pick just like a paid one. That is why the eggs and chests are still worth watching once the feature has started.

The stack — free games only

Once the free games start the eggs and chests stop being triggers and start being multipliers, and a third symbol appears that exists nowhere else — the X, a coin worth one bet the moment it is drawn.

The eggs and chests multiply together, and they multiply the X's coin along with whatever you caught. Two chests short of nothing is still nothing; three chests and the X on a round that caught nothing is five bets, and every egg and every chest together is ×180.

The stack is whatever that draw put on the board. It does not carry from one free game to the next — each one starts again.

The maximum still applies

A free game is a game round, so the $800.00 limit is on it too, and the pick lands on top of whatever the round already paid.

Payback

—

The theoretical return of the exact game you have set up — this bet, this many marks. It is the long-run share of everything wagered that comes back as wins, and it already counts the eggs, the lair and the maximum award.

It moves when you change your bet or your number of marks, because those are different games with different tables.

A bigger bet pays back more. The return climbs with every step up the bet ladder — from about 86% at the smallest bet to about 97% at the largest. That is the same wager buying a better game, not just bigger numbers, and this meter is where you can see it.

Speed and sound

SPEED sets how fast the coins land — slowest, slow, normal, fast, turbo. Turbo drops the whole draw in about a quarter of a second and lets the misses fall quiet, so only your hits speak. SOUND steps through off, quiet, normal and loud. Both are remembered, both are yours, and neither touches what anything pays.

Meters

Kept across sessions on this machine, the way a cabinet keeps its hard meters.

Measured return is what has actually come back so far. Over a short run it will sit well above or well below theoretical — that gap is the game, and it closes only over a very large number of plays.
